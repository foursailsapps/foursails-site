Heartcount privacy policy

Effective 23 October 2026. Applies to the Heartcount app for iOS and Android, published by Four Sails.

The short version: Heartcount keeps everything you enter on your own device. We do not run accounts, we do not collect analytics, we show no ads, and we never sell data. If you choose to share with your partner, the items you share are also kept on our sync server, end-to-end encrypted so that only your two phones can read them; nothing is sent until you pair. The only other third parties are the company that handles in-app purchases and the crash-reporting service, which hears from the app only when something goes wrong.

What the app stores, and where

Heartcount saves the information you enter in local storage inside the app's private storage on your phone. That includes:

  • The two names and the date you got together.
  • Dates you add: title, date, whether it repeats yearly, and whether to remind you.
  • Other things you count on this phone: title, start date and whether the start date counts as day 1. These stay on this phone and are never shared with your partner.
  • Settings such as your reminder time (these stay on each phone and are never shared).
  • If you add one, a background photo: a copy of the picture you choose, saved in the app's storage on this phone. It is shown on Home and on your widget, and is never shared with your partner, uploaded, or included in exports. It appears on a milestone card only if you share one (below).

None of this leaves your device unless you export it yourself or turn on sharing (below).

Your phone's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include the app's data, under Apple's or Google's terms, if you have backups turned on.

Sharing with your partner (optional)

Heartcount works entirely on your phone until you choose to share. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the app does not contact our server, until you open Share with partner and show an invite, or open one from your partner.

When you do, the app signs in to our sync server anonymously: it gets a random account ID with no name, email or phone number attached. From then on the server keeps an encrypted copy of what you share so both phones stay in step: the two names, the date you got together, the dates you add (title, date and the yearly and reminder switches), and whether to count the first day as day 1. Per-phone settings such as reminder times are never shared.

The sync server is a database run for us by Supabase in the United States. Everything you share is encrypted on your phone before it is sent (end-to-end, AES-256-GCM), with a key that only your two phones have: it is created when one of you shows an invite and reaches the other phone inside the invite's QR code or link, never through our server. Neither we nor Supabase can read your shared items. The server does see what it needs to keep the phones in step: the anonymous account IDs, which two are paired, the name of each shared item (such as "profile"), its size and when it last changed. Database access rules also ensure that only the two paired phones can read or change the encrypted items. If you send the invite as a link, it passes through the app you send it with (for example Messages), so send it only to your partner. Like any internet service, Supabase sees the IP address your phone connects from and keeps standard request logs for a limited time; see the Supabase privacy policy. An invite expires after 10 minutes.

Tap Stop sharing on either phone to leave the pair; that phone keeps its own copy. The other phone leaves the pair too the next time it opens Heartcount (or comes back to it) and also keeps its own copy; the pair and every shared item are then deleted from the server. Settings › Delete everything on this phone stops sharing first, so your partner keeps their copy. To have the anonymous account ID removed as well, email us.

Exports you choose to share

With Pro you can export your dates, and the other things you count on this phone, as a JSON file from Settings. An export happens only when you tap the button; you pick where the file goes using your phone's share sheet, and whatever app or service you send it to handles it under its own policy.

A milestone card (your names and your count, on your photo if you added one) is made on your phone as an image and leaves it only if you send it through the share sheet.

Settings › Restore from backup reads a backup file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker and replaces the app's data on this device with it. The file is read only on your phone.

Notifications

Reminders for anniversaries, milestones and your own dates are local notifications scheduled on your phone; no push server is involved. The app asks for permission when you first turn reminders on, and you can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings.

Home-screen widget

The home-screen widget shows your day count and the next dates, or one of the other things you count if you pick it in Settings. To draw it, the app saves a short summary (names, start date, the next two dates and, if you added one, your photo, or that counter's title and count) in storage shared only with its own widget on your phone. It never leaves the device.

App updates

To receive bug fixes without a full App Store update, Heartcount checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo, 650 Industries) when it starts. That request contains the app's version, your platform and an update channel name, and Expo's servers see your IP address as with any download. It carries none of the data you enter in the app and no identifier that links to you. Expo's handling is described in the Expo privacy policy.

Crash reports

If Heartcount crashes or runs into an error, it sends a crash report to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), the service we use to find and fix bugs. A report holds the technical details of the problem: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, and facts about the phone at that moment (model, operating system, language, free memory and storage), plus a random installation ID created by the crash reporter that is not linked to your name, email or any account. It does not include the entries, notes or other information you keep in the app; before a report leaves your phone, the app also removes user details, the device's name, log lines and the addresses of web requests. Nothing is sent while the app works normally: no usage tracking, no screenshots, no performance monitoring. Sentry does not store your IP address with these reports and deletes them automatically within 90 days. Sentry's handling is described in the Sentry privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Paid features are sold through the App Store and Google Play. To unlock them and to restore purchases, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app-user identifier (a random ID generated on your device, not linked to your name or email) and purchase receipt information from the store. It uses this to confirm which features you have paid for. RevenueCat's handling of that data is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. Apple and Google process the payment itself under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details.

On iPhone and iPad, the app also asks Apple for an anonymous attribution token and passes it to RevenueCat. If you installed the app from an ad in App Store search results (Apple Search Ads), this tells us which ad campaign it came from; otherwise it says nothing. The token contains no personal information and is not used to track you across other companies' apps or websites.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking in this version of the app.
  • No advertising or ad networks.
  • No accounts, sign-in or email collection (sharing uses an anonymous ID only).
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Children

Heartcount is a general-purpose app and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; as described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Heartcount removes everything stored on your phone. You can also delete individual items inside the app, or everything at once with Settings › Delete everything on this phone, which stops sharing first. If you were sharing, items you shared stay on your partner's phone. The server copy, which only your two phones could read, is deleted once both phones have left the pair: after you tap Stop sharing, your partner's phone leaves the next time it opens the app. If you uninstall without stopping sharing first, the server copy stays until your partner stops sharing too. If you exported a file, delete that file wherever you saved it. Crash reports are deleted automatically within 90 days. Apart from those, the one thing held off your device is the anonymous purchase record described under In-app purchases. To have it deleted, email support@foursailsapps.com from the device you bought on, name the app, and we will ask RevenueCat to erase the record for that device within 30 days; your store receipt with Google or Apple is unaffected.

Future changes

If a future version adds features that move data off the device, we will update this policy first and tell you inside the app before anything is sent. Features like that will be opt-in. The effective date at the top of this page changes whenever the policy does.
