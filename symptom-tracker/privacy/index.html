Flareday privacy policy

Effective 23 October 2026. Applies to the Flareday app for iOS and Android, published by Four Sails.

The short version: Flareday keeps everything you enter on your own device. We do not run accounts, we do not collect analytics, we show no ads, and we never sell data. The only other third parties are the company that handles in-app purchases and the crash-reporting service, which hears from the app only when something goes wrong.

What the app stores, and where

Flareday saves the information you enter in a local database (SQLite) inside the app's private storage on your phone. That includes:

  • Symptom entries: the symptom name you type, severity, date and time, how long the episode lasted if you enter it, triggers and notes.
  • Medication entries: name, dose, date and time, and notes.
  • The symptom, trigger and medication names you have used, so the app can suggest them again.
  • The dates of doctor visits you add.
  • If you turn on sleep and steps (below), each day's sleep time and step total.
  • Settings such as the daily check-in time.

None of this leaves your device unless you export it yourself. We have no server that receives it, and we cannot see it.

Your phone's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include the app's data, under Apple's or Google's terms, if you have backups turned on.

Health information

What you log in Flareday is health information. It is stored only on your phone. It is not written to Apple Health or Health Connect, not sent to us, and not shared with any third party (RevenueCat, described below, receives purchase information only, never your entries). A summary, PDF or CSV leaves your phone only when you share it yourself.

Sleep and steps from Apple Health and Health Connect (optional)

If you turn on Sleep and steps in Settings (off by default), Flareday asks for permission to read, and then reads on your phone, only while the app is open:

  • On iPhone: Sleep Analysis and Step Count from Apple Health.
  • On Android: sleep sessions and steps from Health Connect.

The first time it reads the last 180 days, then the days since its last read. It keeps only one line per day, the minutes asleep and the step total, in its local database, to show them next to your entries and compare how your symptoms go after short nights or low-step days. These daily totals are not included in the JSON backup; if you share a summary or PDF report while the setting is on, its comparisons can include them. Flareday never writes anything to Apple Health or Health Connect, and the data is never sent to us, shared with anyone, or used for advertising.

Use of information received from Health Connect follows the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Turning the setting off stops the reads and deletes the stored daily totals. You can also withdraw access at any time: on iPhone in the Health app (Sharing › Apps › Flareday), on Android in Health Connect's app permissions.

Exports you choose to share

With Pro you can share a text summary or a PDF visit report of the last 90 days, or export entries and medications as CSV files, from Settings; anyone can export a JSON backup (your entries, medications, suggestions and visit dates). The PDF is created on your phone. An export happens only when you tap the button; you pick where the file goes using your phone's share sheet, and whatever app or service you send it to handles it under its own policy.

Settings › Restore from backup reads a backup file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker and replaces the app's data on this device with it. The file is read only on your phone.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily check-in or the "Log today's meds" reminder, the app schedules one local notification a day for each, at the times you choose; no push server is involved. The reminders never contain medication names or doses. Both are off by default, and you can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings.

Widget

The widget (on the Home Screen, and on iPhone also the Lock Screen) shows only your logging streak, how many days you logged this week, your best streak and how long ago you last logged. It never shows symptom names, severities, triggers, notes or medications, and while App lock is on it shows only neutral text. To draw it, the app saves that short summary in storage shared only with its own widget on your phone (an App Group on iPhone, the app's private files on Android). It never leaves the device.

App updates

To receive bug fixes without a full App Store update, Flareday checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo, 650 Industries) when it starts. That request contains the app's version, your platform and an update channel name, and Expo's servers see your IP address as with any download. It carries none of the data you enter in the app and no identifier that links to you. Expo's handling is described in the Expo privacy policy.

App lock

If you turn on App lock in Settings, the app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode when it opens. The check is done by your phone's operating system; the app only learns whether it succeeded and never sees or stores biometric data.

Crash reports

If Flareday crashes or runs into an error, it sends a crash report to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), the service we use to find and fix bugs. A report holds the technical details of the problem: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, and facts about the phone at that moment (model, operating system, language, free memory and storage), plus a random installation ID created by the crash reporter that is not linked to your name, email or any account. It does not include the entries, notes or other information you keep in the app; before a report leaves your phone, the app also removes user details, the device's name, log lines and the addresses of web requests. Nothing is sent while the app works normally: no usage tracking, no screenshots, no performance monitoring. Sentry does not store your IP address with these reports and deletes them automatically within 90 days. Sentry's handling is described in the Sentry privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Paid features are sold through the App Store and Google Play. To unlock them and to restore purchases, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app-user identifier (a random ID generated on your device, not linked to your name or email) and purchase receipt information from the store. It uses this to confirm which features you have paid for. RevenueCat's handling of that data is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. Apple and Google process the payment itself under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details.

On iPhone and iPad, the app also asks Apple for an anonymous attribution token and passes it to RevenueCat. If you installed the app from an ad in App Store search results (Apple Search Ads), this tells us which ad campaign it came from; otherwise it says nothing. The token contains no personal information and is not used to track you across other companies' apps or websites.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking in this version of the app.
  • No advertising or ad networks.
  • No accounts, sign-in or email collection.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Children

Flareday is a general-purpose app and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; as described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Because the data is stored only on your device, uninstalling Flareday removes all of it. You can also delete individual records inside the app, or everything at once from Settings. If you exported a file, delete that file wherever you saved it. There is nothing for us to delete on our side, because we never received it. Crash reports are deleted automatically within 90 days. Apart from those, the one thing held off your device is the anonymous purchase record described under In-app purchases. To have it deleted, email support@foursailsapps.com from the device you bought on, name the app, and we will ask RevenueCat to erase the record for that device within 30 days; your store receipt with Google or Apple is unaffected.

Future changes

If a future version adds features that move data off the device, we will update this policy first and tell you inside the app before anything is sent. Features like that will be opt-in. The effective date at the top of this page changes whenever the policy does.
