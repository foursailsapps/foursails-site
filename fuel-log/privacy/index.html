Gauge privacy policy

Effective 23 October 2026. Applies to the Gauge app for iOS and Android, published by Four Sails.

The short version: Gauge keeps everything you enter on your own device. We do not run accounts, we do not collect analytics, we show no ads, and we never sell data. The only third parties involved are the company that handles in-app purchases, the crash-reporting service (only when something goes wrong) and, when you use the optional vehicle lookups, free US government vehicle databases.

What the app stores, and where

Gauge saves the information you enter in a local database (SQLite) inside the app's private storage on your phone. That includes:

  • Vehicles you add: name, whether it runs on fuel or electricity, and, if you enter them, its VIN, year, make and model, the EPA rating you picked and the recalls last found for it.
  • Fill-ups: date, odometer reading, volume, price, whether the tank was filled, the station you typed, and notes.
  • Photos you attach to fill-ups and costs, such as receipts and invoices, stored as image files in the app's private storage.
  • Service reminders, the service log and other costs you add, such as insurance, repairs, parking and tolls (what, when, how much and at what mileage).
  • Settings such as your preferred units.

None of this leaves your device unless you export it yourself. We have no server that receives it, and we cannot see it.

Your phone's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include the app's data, under Apple's or Google's terms, if you have backups turned on.

Photos and receipt scanning

You can attach photos to a fill-up or a cost, taken with the camera or picked from your photo library. The app asks for camera access the first time you take one; the photo library opens through the system picker, so Gauge only sees the photos you choose. "Scan receipt" reads the amounts on a receipt photo with the text recognition built into your phone (Apple Vision on iPhone, Google ML Kit's on-device model on Android). The photo and the text are processed only on your phone and are never uploaded. Photos stay in the app's storage until you remove them or delete the fill-up or cost, and they are included in the JSON backup you export.

Vehicle lookups

Three optional features look things up on free public services run by the US government. None of them needs an account, and none receives anything you log in the app (fill-ups, costs, photos, notes).

  • VIN lookup. When you enter a VIN and tap "Look up VIN", Gauge sends the VIN to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's vehicle decoder (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) to fill in the year, make and model. When you open "EPA rating" for a vehicle with a VIN, the VIN is sent to the same decoder again to suggest the matching version.
  • Recalls. For a vehicle with a year, make and model, Gauge asks NHTSA's recall service (api.nhtsa.gov) for the recalls listed for that year, make and model, when you open the vehicle and at most once a week, or when you tap "Check again". Only the year, make and model are sent, never the VIN.
  • EPA rating. When you open "EPA rating", Gauge asks fueleconomy.gov (US Department of Energy and EPA) for the versions of that year, make and model and for the rating of the version you pick. Only the year, make, model and the chosen version are sent.

As with any web request, these services see your IP address. Their handling is described in the US Department of Transportation privacy policy (NHTSA) and the US Department of Energy privacy policy (fueleconomy.gov). If you never enter a VIN, year, make or model, Gauge makes none of these requests.

Widget and shortcuts

The home-screen widget shows the next service, your latest efficiency and this month's spend. The app writes that summary to storage shared only with its own widget on your phone (an App Group on iPhone, the app's private files on Android); it is not sent anywhere. "Log fuel" and "Scan receipt" from Siri, the Shortcuts app or the Action Button only open the app: if you use Siri, Apple processes your voice request under Apple's privacy policy, and Gauge receives just the action, not the recording.

Importing a file

Settings › Import fill-ups reads a CSV file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker (for example an export from Gauge or Fuelly) and adds its fill-ups to the app. The file is read only on your phone.

Exports you choose to share

You can export your fill-ups and your services and costs as CSV files, or everything (attached photos included) as a JSON file, from Settings. An export happens only when you tap the export button; you pick where the file goes using your phone's share sheet, and whatever app or service you send it to handles it under its own policy.

Settings › Restore from backup reads a backup file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker and replaces the app's data on this device with it. The file is read only on your phone.

Notifications

Service reminders set by date are local notifications on the due date. The app offers to turn notifications on during setup (you can say not now) and asks again only when you set up a reminder, and you can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings. Reminders are scheduled locally on the device; no push server is involved.

App updates

To receive bug fixes without a full App Store update, Gauge checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo, 650 Industries) when it starts. That request contains the app's version, your platform and an update channel name, and Expo's servers see your IP address as with any download. It carries none of the data you enter in the app and no identifier that links to you. Expo's handling is described in the Expo privacy policy.

Crash reports

If Gauge crashes or runs into an error, it sends a crash report to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), the service we use to find and fix bugs. A report holds the technical details of the problem: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, and facts about the phone at that moment (model, operating system, language, free memory and storage), plus a random installation ID created by the crash reporter that is not linked to your name, email or any account. It does not include the entries, notes or other information you keep in the app; before a report leaves your phone, the app also removes user details, the device's name, log lines and the addresses of web requests. Nothing is sent while the app works normally: no usage tracking, no screenshots, no performance monitoring. Sentry does not store your IP address with these reports and deletes them automatically within 90 days. Sentry's handling is described in the Sentry privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Paid features are sold through the App Store and Google Play. To unlock them and to restore purchases, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app-user identifier (a random ID generated on your device, not linked to your name or email) and purchase receipt information from the store. It uses this to confirm which features you have paid for. RevenueCat's handling of that data is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. Apple and Google process the payment itself under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details.

On iPhone and iPad, the app also asks Apple for an anonymous attribution token and passes it to RevenueCat. If you installed the app from an ad in App Store search results (Apple Search Ads), this tells us which ad campaign it came from; otherwise it says nothing. The token contains no personal information and is not used to track you across other companies' apps or websites.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking in this version of the app.
  • No advertising or ad networks.
  • No accounts, sign-in or email collection.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Children

Gauge is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; as described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Because the data is stored only on your device, uninstalling Gauge removes all of it. You can also delete individual records inside the app. If you exported a file, delete that file wherever you saved it. There is nothing for us to delete on our side, because we never received it. Crash reports are deleted automatically within 90 days. Apart from those, the one thing held off your device is the anonymous purchase record described under In-app purchases. To have it deleted, email support@foursailsapps.com from the device you bought on, name the app, and we will ask RevenueCat to erase the record for that device within 30 days; your store receipt with Google or Apple is unaffected.

Future changes

If a future version adds other optional features that move data off the device, such as syncing or pairing between two phones, we will update this policy first and tell you inside the app before anything is sent. Features like that will be opt-in. The effective date at the top of this page changes whenever the policy does.
