Splashlab privacy policy

Effective 23 October 2026. Applies to the Splashlab app for iOS and Android, published by Four Sails.

The short version: Splashlab keeps everything you enter on your own device. We do not run accounts, we do not collect analytics, we show no ads, and we never sell data. The only third parties involved are the company that handles in-app purchases and the crash-reporting service, which hears from the app only when something goes wrong.

What the app stores, and where

Splashlab saves the information you enter in a local database (SQLite) inside the app's private storage on your phone. That includes:

  • Pool and hot tub profiles: name, volume, surface type, sanitizer type, any custom target ranges and related settings.
  • Test readings you enter (free chlorine, combined chlorine, pH, alkalinity, calcium, CYA, salt, bromine) with their dates.
  • The chemicals you mark as added (product, amount, date and, if you entered prices, what that addition cost).
  • The chemical prices you enter.
  • Maintenance tasks and the log of what you did (brushing, vacuuming, skimming, backwashing, filter cleaning and filter pressure readings), with notes.
  • SLAM runs with their chlorine checks, and overnight chlorine loss tests.
  • Test and maintenance reminder schedules.
  • Settings such as your preferred units.

None of this leaves your device unless you export it yourself. We have no server that receives it, and we cannot see it.

Your phone's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include the app's data, under Apple's or Google's terms, if you have backups turned on.

Scanning readings

"Scan readings" on the test form fills in values from a screenshot or a photo, for example of a test strip app or a pool store printout. The app asks for camera access the first time you take a photo; the photo library opens through the system picker, so Splashlab only sees the image you choose. The text is read on your phone by the text recognition built into it (Apple Vision on iPhone, Google ML Kit's on-device model, bundled with the app, on Android). Splashlab keeps only the values you save with the test: the image and its text are not stored by the app and never uploaded.

Exports you choose to share

You can export everything listed above as a JSON backup from Settings, and with Pro your tests and chemical log (with costs) as CSV files. An export happens only when you tap the export button; you pick where the file goes using your phone's share sheet, and whatever app or service you send it to handles it under its own policy.

Settings › Restore from backup reads a backup file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker and replaces the app's data on this device with it. The file is read only on your phone.

Notifications

If you turn on test or maintenance reminders, the app asks for permission to show notifications so it can remind you when a test or task is due. The permission request appears only when you set up a reminder, and you can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings. Reminders are scheduled locally on the device; no push server is involved.

Home-screen widget

The widget (on the Home Screen, and on iPhone also the Lock Screen) shows your most recently tested pool: its name, how long ago you tested, and the latest chlorine (or bromine) and pH against their targets. To draw it, the app saves that short summary in storage shared only with its own widget on your phone (an App Group on iPhone, the app's private files on Android). It never leaves the device.

App updates

To receive bug fixes without a full App Store update, Splashlab checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo, 650 Industries) when it starts. That request contains the app's version, your platform and an update channel name, and Expo's servers see your IP address as with any download. It carries none of the data you enter in the app and no identifier that links to you. Expo's handling is described in the Expo privacy policy.

Crash reports

If Splashlab crashes or runs into an error, it sends a crash report to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), the service we use to find and fix bugs. A report holds the technical details of the problem: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, and facts about the phone at that moment (model, operating system, language, free memory and storage), plus a random installation ID created by the crash reporter that is not linked to your name, email or any account. It does not include the entries, notes or other information you keep in the app; before a report leaves your phone, the app also removes user details, the device's name, log lines and the addresses of web requests. Nothing is sent while the app works normally: no usage tracking, no screenshots, no performance monitoring. Sentry does not store your IP address with these reports and deletes them automatically within 90 days. Sentry's handling is described in the Sentry privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Paid features are sold through the App Store and Google Play. To unlock them and to restore purchases, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app-user identifier (a random ID generated on your device, not linked to your name or email) and purchase receipt information from the store. It uses this to confirm which features you have paid for. RevenueCat's handling of that data is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. Apple and Google process the payment itself under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details.

On iPhone and iPad, the app also asks Apple for an anonymous attribution token and passes it to RevenueCat. If you installed the app from an ad in App Store search results (Apple Search Ads), this tells us which ad campaign it came from; otherwise it says nothing. The token contains no personal information and is not used to track you across other companies' apps or websites.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking in this version of the app.
  • No advertising or ad networks.
  • No accounts, sign-in or email collection.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Children

Splashlab is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; as described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Because the data is stored only on your device, uninstalling Splashlab removes all of it. You can also delete individual records inside the app. If you exported a file, delete that file wherever you saved it. There is nothing for us to delete on our side, because we never received it. Crash reports are deleted automatically within 90 days. Apart from those, the one thing held off your device is the anonymous purchase record described under In-app purchases. To have it deleted, email support@foursailsapps.com from the device you bought on, name the app, and we will ask RevenueCat to erase the record for that device within 30 days; your store receipt with Google or Apple is unaffected.

Future changes

If a future version adds optional features that move data off the device, such as syncing or pairing between two phones, we will update this policy first and tell you inside the app before anything is sent. Features like that will be opt-in. The effective date at the top of this page changes whenever the policy does.
