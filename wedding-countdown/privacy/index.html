I Do privacy policy

Effective 20 October 2026. Applies to the I Do app for iOS and Android, published by Four Sails.

The short version: I Do keeps everything you enter on your own device. We do not run accounts, we do not collect analytics, we show no ads, and we never sell data. If you choose to share with your partner, the items you share are also kept on our sync server so both phones match; nothing is sent until you pair. The only other third party is the company that handles in-app purchases.

What the app stores, and where

I Do saves the information you enter in local storage inside the app's private storage on your phone. That includes:

  • The two names, the wedding date and the venue.
  • Checklist tasks: title, due date, category, done or not, and notes.
  • Vendors: name, category, the contact details you type, cost, booked status and notes.
  • Settings such as your reminder time and currency (these stay on each phone and are never shared).

None of this leaves your device unless you export it yourself or turn on sharing (below).

Your phone's own backup (for example iCloud or Google backup) may include the app's data, under Apple's or Google's terms, if you have backups turned on.

Sharing with your partner (optional)

I Do works entirely on your phone until you choose to share. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the app does not contact our server, until you open Share with partner and create or enter a six-digit code.

When you do, the app signs in to our sync server anonymously: it gets a random account ID with no name, email or phone number attached. From then on the server keeps a copy of what you share so both phones stay in step: the two names, the wedding date and venue, your checklist tasks, and your vendors (including the contact details, costs and notes you type). Per-phone settings such as reminder times are never shared.

The sync server is a database run for us by Supabase in the United States. Database access rules ensure that only the two paired phones can read or change your shared items. We do not look at them, except to troubleshoot when you ask us to. Like any internet service, Supabase sees the IP address your phone connects from and keeps standard request logs for a limited time; see the Supabase privacy policy. A pairing code expires after 10 minutes.

Tap Stop sharing on either phone to leave the pair; that phone keeps its own copy. When both phones have left, the pair and every shared item are deleted from the server. To have the anonymous account ID removed as well, email us.

Exports you choose to share

With Pro you can export everything as a JSON file from Settings. An export happens only when you tap the button; you pick where the file goes using your phone's share sheet, and whatever app or service you send it to handles it under its own policy.

Settings › Restore from backup reads a backup file that you pick yourself with your phone's file picker and replaces the app's data on this device with it. The file is read only on your phone.

Notifications

Reminders for tasks and countdown milestones are local notifications scheduled on your phone; no push server is involved. The app asks for permission when you first turn reminders on, and you can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings.

Home-screen widget

The home-screen widget shows the countdown and your next tasks. To draw it, the app saves a short summary (names, date, venue and the next two tasks) in storage shared only with its own widget on your phone. It never leaves the device.

App updates

To receive bug fixes without a full App Store update, I Do checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo, 650 Industries) when it starts. That request contains the app's version, your platform and an update channel name, and Expo's servers see your IP address as with any download. It carries none of the data you enter in the app and no identifier that links to you. Expo's handling is described in the Expo privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Paid features are sold through the App Store and Google Play as a one-time purchase. To unlock them and to restore purchases, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app-user identifier (a random ID generated on your device, not linked to your name or email) and purchase receipt information from the store. It uses this to confirm which features you have paid for. RevenueCat's handling of that data is described in the RevenueCat privacy policy. Apple and Google process the payment itself under their own privacy policies; we never see your payment details.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking in this version of the app.
  • No advertising or ad networks.
  • No accounts, sign-in or email collection (sharing uses an anonymous ID only).
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Children

I Do is a general-purpose app and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; as described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling I Do removes everything stored on your phone. You can also delete individual items inside the app. If you were sharing, items you shared stay on your partner's phone and on the server until both phones have stopped sharing; then the server copy is deleted. If you exported a file, delete that file wherever you saved it.

Future changes

If a future version adds features that move data off the device, we will update this policy first and tell you inside the app before anything is sent. Features like that will be opt-in. The effective date at the top of this page changes whenever the policy does.
